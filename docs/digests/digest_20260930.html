🚀 Opportunity of the Day2 min read

The single best thing to build right now.

Agentic-Skill-Registry-and-Optimizer (ASRO)

  • The gap: Current agentic systems treat "skills" (tool-use patterns) as ephemeral prompt-injected instructions, leading to massive redundant planning and "skill drift" across different harnesses, as highlighted in the Retrieval-Augmented Skill Optimization paper.
  • Why now: The convergence of the MCP spec for tool interoperability and the release of GPT-6.1 Sol makes it economically and technically feasible to maintain a centralized, versioned registry of optimized tool-use trajectories.
  • Build as: An OSS middleware library that sits between the Agent Harness and the LLM, providing a "Skill-Cache" that intercepts tool-call sequences and injects optimized, pre-validated skill-trajectories.
  • The hard part: Semantic deduplication of tool-use trajectories; the system must recognize that two different prompt-sequences are functionally identical and map them to a single, optimized skill-artifact.
  • Wedge & moat: The wedge is a "Skill-Linter" for existing LangChain/AutoGen projects; the moat is the accumulated library of validated, high-performance skill-trajectories that improve in accuracy and latency the more they are used across an enterprise.
  • Already heating up: The Retrieval-Augmented Skill Optimization paper (Sep 2026) and the rapid adoption of MCP servers indicate a clear market shift toward standardized tool-use.
  • Closest existing solutions: LangGraph (focuses on state machines, not skill-trajectory optimization), Mem0 (focuses on user-memory, not tool-use skill optimization).
  • First step this week: Prototype a "Skill-Interceptor" that logs tool-call traces from a standard LangGraph agent and generates a JSON-based "Skill-Artifact" for the most frequent 5% of tasks.

🔬 Deep Reads — For When You Have Time (skip if rushed)

The one paper to actually read this week.

📖 The One Deep Read

Follow the Entities: A Corpus Map for Agentic Search by Jeong and Jauhar. This paper is essential for anyone building RAG or agentic search systems; it moves beyond simple vector retrieval to a graph-based "corpus map" that connects evidence across documents. Read it to understand how to solve the "fragmented evidence" problem that plagues current enterprise agents.

Read it for: The methodology for building a persistent, entity-aware knowledge graph that agents can traverse to complete complex, multi-document tasks.
